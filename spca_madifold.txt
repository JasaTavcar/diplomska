Supervised Principal Component Analysis Via Manifold Optimization

There are two kinds of SPCA algorithms:
- two step: first PCA, then supervised (or vice versa)
    - Bair's method
- single step: the tradeoff between variance explained and prediction error is explicitly addressed by a single param
    - manifold Optimization
    - multiobjective approach

Stiefel manifold: set of matrices with orhonormal columns.
    - geometrically, this is a curved space
    - why do we care?
        * when you do a gradient descent step, LLt => (L-xG)(L-xG)t, orthonormality breaks,
        * we do the gradient step on the tangent space of the manifold space
        * then we need to project it back onto the manifold space

Grassmann manifold G(p, k): all k-dimensional subspaces in p-dimensional space
    - G(3,2) = all planes through the origin in 3D - each point in the manifold is one plane.
    - this is different then Steifel, since any subscpace can be spanned by infinitely many matrices.
    - we use Grassmann instead, since it's possible to prove,
        that prediction will be the same, no matter which L we choose among the ones that span the same subspace
    - for proof, apply the same rotation to beta and L matrices, the result doesn't change
        -> i.e. rotating L can be compensated by rotating beta
    - since many operations (gradient, tangents, moving along godisics) are easier when 
        you remove the redundant degrees of freedom, our search space will be the Grassmann manifold.

Riemmann manifold = manifold with geometry defined (Grassmann manifold is a r.m.) 

Regularization is unnecessary
    * regression with beta coefficients is performed on the reduced matrix L.
    * regularization is a tool to prevent overfitting on complex data.
    * here the data representatioin is inherently simple

Why solve the least squares error linear regression with MP inverse?
    * MP inverse optimally solves LSE
    * Replaces beta with L in equation, now we only have to minimize one variable
        - although this makes calculating derivatives harder

Gradient descent algorithm:
    * Gradient step = step in the direction of the gradient
        - does not stay on the Grassmann manifold
    * Better gradient step = compure grad -> project to tangent space -> tangent step -> retraction
        - retraction = correction after a tangent step to stay on the curved surface
    * Geodesic step = direst step that stays on the curved surface
        - intuition: performed by rotating the surface
        - compute the SVD of Riemannian gradient
        - rotate along the "principal rotation directons" (orthogonal rotation components). 
    * Armijo backtracking: choice of step size
        - if the step size does not lower the loss (enough), shrink it.
    * The stopping confition is grad(L)_f < eps, where grad(L) is Riemannian gradient,
        the gradient of the projection onto the tangent space.
